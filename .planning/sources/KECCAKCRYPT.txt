<h1 class="page-title">Third-party cryptanalysis</h1>
</div>
</header>
<div class="container">
<p>This page lists all the third-party cryptanalysis results that we know of on <span class="sc">Keccak</span> and <span class="sc">Xoodoo</span>, including FIPS&#160;202, SP&#160;800-185 and RFC&#160;9861 instances (SHA-3, SHAKE, TurboSHAKE, <span class="sc">KangarooTwelve</span>), as well as <span class="sc">Ketje</span>, <span class="sc">Keyak</span>, <span class="sc">Kravatte</span>, <span class="sc">Xoofff</span> and <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span>. We may have forgotten some results, so if you think your result is relevant and should be on this page, please do not hesitate to contact us.</p>

<p>The results are divided into the following categories:</p>
<ul>
    <li>analysis of <span class="sc">Keccak</span> (covering also FIPS&#160;202, SP&#160;800-185 and RFC&#160;9861 instances) and <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span> in the context of (unkeyed) hashing;</li>
    <li>analysis that is more specifically targetting keyed modes of use of <span class="sc">Keccak</span> and <span class="sc">Xoodoo</span>-based schemes, including KMAC, <span class="sc">Ketje</span>, <span class="sc">Keyak</span>, <span class="sc">Kravatte</span>, <span class="sc">Xoofff</span> and <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span>;</li>
    <li>analysis on the (reduced-round) <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> and <span class="sc">Xoodoo</span> permutations that does not extend to any of the aforementioned cryptographic functions.</li>
</ul>

<p>In each category, the most recent results come first.</p>

<h1>Analysis of unkeyed modes</h1>
<p>First, the <a href="crunchy_contest.html">Crunchy Crypto Collision and Pre-image Contest</a> contains third-party cryptanalysis results with practical complexities.</p>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_tosc_LinLSRYDW26">X. Lin, Z. Lu, Y. Shen, C. Ren, H. Yu, E. Dong and W. Wu, <b>Practical Preimage Attack on 5-Round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>[r=640, c=160]</b>, IACR Trans. Symmetric Cryptol., 2026 <a href="https://tosc.iacr.org/index.php/ToSC/article/view/12783"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.46586/tosc.v2026.i1.189-211"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/tosc/LinLSRYDW26"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Xiaoen Lin, Zhengrong Lu, Yantian Shen, Chongxu Ren, Hongbo Yu, Enming Dong and Wenhao Wu achieve a significant cryptanalytic milestone by presenting the first fully practical preimage attack on a 5-round variant of <span class="sc">Keccak</span> with a 160-bit capacity. By combining advanced SAT-solver modeling with an optimized conditional bit-fixing strategy, they successfully identify and exploit deep algebraic vulnerabilities in the permutation's early round transitions. This technique was used to solve a concrete instance of the <a href="crunchy_contest.html">Crunchy Pre-image Contest</a> on 5-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=640, <i>c</i>=160] with a complexity of 2<sup>59.7</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_dcc_HeLY26">L. He, X. Lin and H. Yu, <b>Improved preimage attacks on round-reduced <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-384/512</b>, Des. Codes Cryptogr., 2026 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/788"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-025-01780-z"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/dcc/HeLY26"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Le He, Xiaoen Lin and Hongbo Yu improve the complexity of preimage attacks against round-reduced <span class="sc">Keccak</span> with a high capacity (<i>c</i>=768 or 1024). For instance, the complexity of a preimage attack on <span class="sc">Keccak</span>[<i>r</i>=832, <i>c</i>=768] reduced to 2 rounds is now 2<sup>28</sup> (instead of 2<sup>89</sup> previously). On other instances, the complexity remains theoretical, e.g., for <span class="sc">Keccak</span>[<i>r</i>=576, <i>c</i>=1024] reduced to 3 rounds the complexity is now 2<sup>426</sup> (instead of 2<sup>475</sup> previously).</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_dcc_LinHLSRY25">X. Lin, L. He, Z. Lu, Y. Shen, C. Ren and H. Yu, <b>Internal differential structure: preimage attacks on up to 5-round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span></b>, Des. Codes Cryptogr., 2025 <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-025-01655-3"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-025-01655-3"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/dcc/LinHLSRY25"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Xiaoen Lin, Le He, Zhengrong Lu, Yantian Shen, Chongxu Ren and Hongbo Yu formalize a generalized cryptanalytic framework known as the internal differential structure to enhance preimage attacks on round-reduced <span class="sc">Keccak</span>. By mapping highly organized propagation paths for internal differences across multiple rounds, they establish a rigid algebraic subspace that maximizes message-modification degrees of freedom while isolating targeted output bits. This structural optimization enables them to bypass previous diffusion obstacles, delivering state-of-the-art theoretical and practical preimage attacks on 5-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=1088, <i>c</i>=512] with a complexity of 2<sup>230</sup>. Additionally, this technique was used to solve concrete instances of the <a href="crunchy_contest.html">Crunchy Pre-image Contest</a> on 4-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=240, <i>c</i>=160] with complexity 2<sup>58.7</sup> and on 2-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=40, <i>c</i>=160] with complexity 2<sup>49.0</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_tosc_QinZHJWD25">L. Qin, B. Zhao, Q. Hou, K. Jia, G. Wang and X. Dong, <b>Linear Cancellations in the MitM Attacks on Sponge Functions</b>, IACR Trans. Symmetric Cryptol., 2025 <a href="https://tosc.iacr.org/index.php/ToSC/article/view/12481"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.46586/tosc.v2025.i3.633-692"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/tosc/QinZHJWD25"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Lingyue Qin, Boxin Zhao, Qingliang Hou, Keting Jia, Gaoli Wang and Xiaoyang Dong introduce a novel "linear cancellation" technique to systematically optimize meet-in-the-middle (MitM) preimage attacks on sponge-based hash functions. By exploiting the sparse linear relationships within the <span class="sc">Keccak</span> state transitions, this approach allows cryptanalysts to strategically cancel out complex neutral bit dependencies during the forward and backward chunk separation phases. This methodology significantly expands the number of available neutral bits, leading to preimage attacks on 4-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=576, <i>c</i>=1024] with complexity 2<sup>500</sup>, on 4-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=832, <i>c</i>=768] with complexity 2<sup>367</sup> and on 3-round <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span> with complexity 2<sup>121</sup>, as well as a collision attack on 3-round <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span> with complexity 2<sup>60.5</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_iacr_GilkolaeiE25">R. R. Gilkolaei and R. Ebrahimi, <b>Quantum Resource Analysis of Low-Round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>/SHA-3 Preimage Attack: From Classical 2^57.8 to Quantum 2^28.9 using Qiskit Modeling</b>, IACR Cryptol. ePrint Arch., 2025 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2277"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/iacr/GilkolaeiE25"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Ramin Rezvani Gilkolaei and Reza Ebrahimi present a comprehensive quantum resource analysis of preimage attacks on low-round SHA-3 by modeling the attack using IBM's Qiskit framework. By implementing a highly optimized quantum circuit for the <span class="sc">Keccak</span> permutation, they provide concrete metrics for the gate counts, qubits, and circuit depth required to execute a Grover-based search. Their implementation demonstrates that a classical preimage attack complexity of 2<sup>57.8</sup> operations can be successfully reduced to a quantum complexity of 2<sup>28.9</sup> operations, offering an empirical benchmark for evaluating the post-quantum security of reduced-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_sacrypt_LiHQ25">H. Li, L. He and W. Qiu, <b>Practical Collision Attacks on Reduced-Round <span class='sc'>Xoodyak</span> Hash Mode</b>, SAC, 2025 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1430"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-10536-3_3"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/sacrypt/LiHQ25"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Huina Li, Le He and Weidong Qiu introduce a new multi-block differential cryptanalysis framework specifically tailored to launch the first practical collision attacks on the round-reduced hash mode of the lightweight primitive <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span>. By analyzing the unique properties of the underlying <span class="sc">Xoodoo</span> permutation's linear (<i>θ</i>) and non-linear (<i>χ</i>) layers, they manually design specific symmetric characteristics called "loop differential trails." Utilizing these trails, the authors construct a highly optimized SAT-solver model to efficiently find conforming message pairs, delivering a practical collision on 2-round <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span> and a theoretical collision attack on 3-round <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span> with complexity 2<sup>100.93</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_eurocrypt_ZhangHL25">Z. Zhang, C. Hou and M. Liu, <b>Preimage Attacks on up to 5 Rounds of SHA-3 Using Internal Differentials</b>, Eurocrypt, 2025 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/439"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-91107-1_12"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/eurocrypt/ZhangHL25"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Zhongyi Zhang, Chengan Hou and Meicheng Liu present a breakthrough cryptanalytic technique that extends internal differential analysis from collision generation to full preimage retrieval on round-reduced SHA-3. By constructing a highly structured internal differential trail that spans multiple rounds, they successfully decouple large portions of the state to isolate localized target bits. This strategy allows them to significantly reduce the complexity of matching the hash output, yielding different preimage attacks with complexities ranging from 2<sup>100.5</sup> for 5-round SHAKE128 to 2<sup>254.33</sup> for 5-round SHAKE256.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_dcc_SuryawanshiGSR25">S. Suryawanshi, S. Ghosh, D. Saha and P. Ram, <b>Simple vs. vectorial: exploiting structural symmetry to beat the ZeroSum distinguisher</b>, Des. Codes Cryptogr., 2025 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/052"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-024-01502-x"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/dcc/SuryawanshiGSR25"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Sahiba Suryawanshi, Shibam Ghosh, Dhiman Saha and Prathamesh Ram introduce SymSum_Sim, a novel algebraic distinguisher based on k-fold simple derivatives that overcomes the Maximum Attainable Derivative (MAD) limitations of previous vectorial-derivative approaches. By exploiting structural symmetry within the permutation states, this framework breaks the traditional MAD barrier to significantly outperform the standard ZeroSum distinguisher on round-reduced <span class="sc">Keccak</span> and <span class="sc">Xoodyak</span>. This work also includes 15-round and 16-round distinguishers for the <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>[1600] permutation.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_tosc_LinHY25">X. Lin, L. He and H. Yu, <b>Practical Preimage Attacks on 3-Round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-256 and 4-Round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>[r=640, c=160]</b>, IACR Trans. Symmetric Cryptol., 2025 <a href="https://tosc.iacr.org/index.php/ToSC/article/view/12079"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.46586/tosc.v2025.i1.328-356"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/tosc/LinHY25"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Xiaoen Lin, Le He and Hongbo Yu present new, highly efficient preimage attacks on round-reduced <span class="sc">Keccak</span> variants by introducing a refined MILP-based (Mixed Integer Linear Programming) automated search framework to find optimal internal differential characteristics. By combining this algebraic modeling with a novel conditional bit-fixing strategy, they successfully maximize the available degrees of freedom during the message-filling phase. This approach allows them to achieve a practical preimage attack on 3-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=1088, <i>c</i>=512] with complexity 2<sup>57.2</sup> and on 4-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-[<i>r</i>=640, <i>c</i>=160] with complexity 2<sup>60.9</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_crypto_ZhangHL24">Z. Zhang, C. Hou and M. Liu, <b>Probabilistic Linearization: Internal Differential Collisions in up to 6 Rounds of SHA-3</b>, Crypto, 2024 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1136"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-68385-5_8"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/crypto/ZhangHL24"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Zhongyi Zhang, Chengan Hou and Meicheng Liu introduce a novel cryptanalytic framework called probabilistic linearization to significantly extend the reach of collision attacks on round-reduced SHA-3. By relaxing the strict condition constraints of traditional internal differential attacks, this technique treats certain non-linear state bit conditions probabilistically, allowing them to balance condition fulfillment against a massively expanded space of degrees of freedom. This optimization enables them to construct collision attacks on up to 6 rounds, with a complexity of 2<sup>232.29</sup> on 6-round SHAKE256.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_eurocrypt_ZhangHL23">Z. Zhang, C. Hou and M. Liu, <b>Collision Attacks on Round-Reduced SHA-3 Using Conditional Internal Differentials</b>, Eurocrypt, 2023 <a href="https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-30634-1_8"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-30634-1_8"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/eurocrypt/ZhangHL23"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Zhongyi Zhang, Chengan Hou and Meicheng Liu present an improved collision attack on round-reduced SHA-3 by introducing a new cryptanalytic technique called conditional internal differentials. By carefully managing internal state differences across early rounds, they establish conditional structures that significantly maximize the available degrees of freedom during message modification. This methodology allows them to bypass previous bottleneck constraints, and resulting collisions attacks on 4-round SHA3-512 and 5-round SHAKE256 with complexities of 2<sup>237</sup> and 2<sup>185</sup>, respectively.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">

<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_sss_SuryawanshiS23">S. Suryawanshi and D. Saha, <b>Where Are the Constants? Stanica, <b>Permutation rotation-symmetric Sboxes, liftings and affine equivalence</b>, IACR Cryptol. ePrint Arch., 2022 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/279"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/iacr/OmlandS22"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Tron Omland and Pantelimon Stanica investigate permutation shift-invariant vectorial Boolean functions that generalize <span class="sc">Keccak</span>'s χ mapping.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_iacr_BelliniM22">E. Bellini and R. H. Makarim, <b>Functional Cryptanalysis: Application to reduced-round <span class='sc'>Xoodoo</span></b>, IACR Cryptol. ePrint Arch., 2022 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/134"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/iacr/BelliniM22"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Emanuele Bellini and Rusydi H. Makarim introduce functional cryptanalysis as a generalization of other techniques such as differential and rotational cryptanalysis. They show a functional distinguisher on 3 rounds of <span class="sc">Xoodoo</span>, which works better than a differential distinguisher.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_eurocrypt_LiuSL21">Y. Liu, S. Sun and C. Li, <b>Rotational cryptanalysis from a differential-linear perspective: practical distinguishers for round-reduced FRIET, <span class='sc'>Xoodoo</span>, and Alzette</b>, Eurocrypt, 2021 <a href="https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/189"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-77870-5_26"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/eurocrypt/LiuSL21"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Yunwen Liu, Siwei Sun and Chiao Li revisit the rotational cryptanalysis from the perspective of differential-linear cryptanalysis. They apply this technique to find rotational differential-linear distinguishers with correlation 1 on <span class="sc">Xoodoo</span> reduced to 4 rounds.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_iet-ifs_YanLWYX19">H. Yan, X. Lai, L. Wang, Y. Yu and Y. Xing, <b>New zero-sum distinguishers on full 24-round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> using the division property</b>, IET Inf. Secur., 2019 <a href="https://doi.org/10.1049/iet-ifs.2018.5263"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/iet-ifs/YanLWYX19"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Hailun Yan, Xuejia Lai, Lei Wang, Yu Yu and Yiran Xing propose a new zero-sum distinguisher of full 24-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> by using the <em>division property</em>, a generalized integral property that was initially used in the integral cryptanalysis of symmetric-key algorithms.  Extending on work done by Todo at CRYPTO 2015, they prove a propagation rule of the division property under the assumption that the S-box specification is known to attackers. They apply this to S-boxes in <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> and Ascon.  For full-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>, this brings the best distinguisher to a complexity of 2<sup>1573</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_tosc_LiuQT19">G. Liu, W. Qiu and Y. Tu, <b>New Techniques for Searching Differential Trails in <span class='sc'>Keccak</span></b>, IACR Trans. Symmetric Cryptol., 2019 <a href="https://tosc.iacr.org/index.php/ToSC/article/view/8470"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.13154/tosc.v2019.i4.407-437"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/tosc/LiuQT19"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Guozhen Liu, Weidong Qiu and Yi Tu introduce new algorithms to cover 3-round trail cores in <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> with propagation
weight up to 53.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_cisis_LiC17">M. Li and L. Cheng, <b>Distinguishing Property for Full Round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> Permutation</b>, CISIS, 2017 <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-61566-0_59"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/cisis/LiC17"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Maolin Li and Lu Cheng apparently reduced the size of a zero-sum partition to 2<sup>1573</sup> elements.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_fse_JeanN15">J. Jean and I. Nikolić, <b>Internal Differential Boomerangs: Practical Analysis of the Round-Reduced <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> Permutation</b>, Fast Software Encryption, 2015 <a href="http://eprint.iacr.org/2015/244"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-48116-5_26"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/fse/JeanN15"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Jérémy Jean and Ivica Nikolić introduce the technique of <em>internal differential boomerang distinguishers</em>. They analyze the symmetry inside the permutation and propose distinguishers up to 8 rounds of <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>[1600] with practical complexities.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_africacrypt_KuilaSPC14">S. Kuila, D. Saha, M. Pal and D. R. Chowdhury, <b>Practical Distinguishers against 6-Round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> Exploiting Self-Symmetry</b>, Africacrypt, 2014 <a href="http://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/africacrypt/KuilaSPC14"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-06734-6_6"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/africacrypt/KuilaSPC14"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Sukhendu Kuila, Dhiman Saha, Madhumangal Pal and Dipanwita Roy Chowdhury consider distinguishers on <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>[1600] where the state is self-symmetric, i.e., it has a period less than 64 along the <i>z</i>-axis. They show distinguishers up to 6 rounds with a complexity of 2<sup>11</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_fse_Duc0P012">A. Duc, J. Guo, T. Peyrin and L. Wei, <b>Unaligned Rebound Attack: Application to <span class='sc'>Keccak</span></b>, Fast Software Encryption, 2012 <a href="http://eprint.iacr.org/2011/420"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-34047-5_23"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/fse/Duc0P012"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>This paper analyzes two aspects of differential cryptanalysis on <span class="sc">Keccak</span>: efficient trails and rebound attacks. In the former, the authors propose a heuristic to build differential trails with a low restriction weight. For <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>[1600], they obtained trails of weight 32, 142 and 709 for 3, 4 and 5 rounds, respectively. In the latter, the paper presents distinguishers making use of the rebound attack for up to 8 rounds of <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>[1600] with a complexity of 2<sup>491</sup>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="journals_iacr_DuanL11">M. Duan and X. Lai, <b>Improved zero-sum distinguisher for full round <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> permutation</b>, IACR Cryptology ePrint Archive, 2011 <a href="http://eprint.iacr.org/2011/023"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/journals/iacr/DuanL11"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>The authors of this paper noted a property of the inverse of the non-linear function <i>χ</i>: while <i>χ</i><sup>-1</sup> has algebraic degree 3, the product of any two output bits also has degree 3. This allows to estimate the degree of the <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> rounds more tightly and to extend the zero-sum distinguisher on <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>[1600] to size 2<sup>1575</sup> for 24 rounds.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_fse_BouraCC11">C. Boura, A. Canteaut and C. De Cannière, <b>Higher-Order Differential Properties of <span class='sc'>Keccak</span> and Luffa</b>, Fast Software Encryption, 2011 <a href="http://eprint.iacr.org/2010/589"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-21702-9_15"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/fse/BouraCC11"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this work, the authors present a new upper bounds for the algebraic degree of iterated permutations. While for a small number of rounds the algebraic degree increases exponentially with the number of rounds, for a large number of rounds the algebraic degree only converges exponentially to the maximum value (permutation width minus 1). This improves upon existing zero-sum distinguishers for several SHA-3 candidates. For <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>, it allows extending the zero-sum distinguishers to the full 24-round version, although the size of the zero-sums is 2<sup>1590</sup> and the distinguisher does not extend to any other type of attack against <span class="sc">Keccak</span>.</p>



</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_sacrypt_BouraC10">C. Boura and A. Canteaut, <b>Zero-Sum Distinguishers for Iterated Permutations and Application to <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> and Hamsi-256</b>, Selected Areas in Cryptography, 2010 <a href="https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00738200"><i class="fa fa-folder-open-o"></i></a> <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-19574-7_1"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/sacrypt/BouraC10"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Christina Boura and Anne Canteaut extend their zero-sum distinguishers to 20 rounds.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="conf_isit_BouraC10">C. Boura and A. Canteaut, <b>A zero-sum property for the <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> permutation with 18 rounds</b>, ISIT, 2010 <a href="http://www-rocq.inria.fr/secret/Anne.Canteaut/Publications/zero_sum.pdf"><i class="fa fa-file-pdf-o"></i></a> <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1109/ISIT.2010.5513442"><i class="fa fa-book"></i></a> <a href="https://dblp.org/rec/html/conf/isit/BouraC10"><i class="fa fa-graduation-cap"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Christina Boura and Anne Canteaut extend the zero-sum distinguisher of Aumasson and Meier to 18 rounds by analyzing the Walsh spectrum of the non-linear part and bounding the degree of the rounds more tightly. The authors won the <a href="2010/prize3_result.html">third prize for the best cryptanalysis</a>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="__AM0sum">J. Aumasson and W. Meier, <b>Zero-sum distinguishers for reduced <span class='sc'>Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> and for the core functions of Luffa and Hamsi</b>, rump session of CHES, 2009 <a href="http://131002.net/data/papers/AM09.pdf"><i class="fa fa-file-pdf-o"></i></a></p>
<p>In this note, Jean-Philippe Aumasson and Willi Meier investigate a new kind of distinguishers called <em>zero-sum distinguishers</em>. In particular, they compute high-order derivatives of the rounds and the inverse rounds of <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>. Starting from the middle, they obtain a set of values whose sum is zero and whose sum of images through reduced-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i> is also zero. This distinguisher is successful up to 16 rounds. The authors did not find a way to use this distinguisher against the <span class="sc">Keccak</span> sponge function, though. The authors won the <a href="2009/prize2_result.html">second prize for the best cryptanalysis</a>.</p>


</div></div>

<div class="panel panel-default">
<div class="panel-body">
<p class="lead lead-sm" id="__AK0sum">J. Aumasson and D. Khovratovich, <b>First Analysis of <span class='sc'>Keccak</span></b>, NIST hash forum, 2009 <a href="http://131002.net/data/papers/AK09.pdf"><i class="fa fa-file-pdf-o"></i></a></p>
<p>In this paper, Jean-Philippe Aumasson and Dmitry Khovratovich look at two possible distinguishers on reduced-round <span class="sc">Keccak</span>-<i>f</i>[1600]. First, cube testers are applied to detect non-ideal behavior in the algebraic description of the permutation. Second, the authors try to solve the constrained-input constrained-output (CICO) problem using automated algebraic techniques. The authors received 25 bottles of Belgian trappist beer as the paper was awarded the <a href="2009/prize_result.html">first prize for the best cryptanalysis</a>. It was presented by Dmitry Khovratovich at the rump session of Eurocrypt 2009 (<a href="http://eurocrypt2009rump.cr.yp.to/c72e20a378faf18f5bd119a674282427.pdf">Beer-recovery analysis</a>).</p>


</div></div></div>
