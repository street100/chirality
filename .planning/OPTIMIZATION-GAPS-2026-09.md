## Where the time and the memory go

The suite measured 357.3 s wall and 282.9 s of CPU on 2026-09-29, and 79.6 s of that CPU is `sys`. Compiling is the smaller share. The wrapper run logged 47.2 s of compile wall over 340 compiles, in a run where 13 rows went red and skipped their mutant legs. Every large cost found outside the compiler is shell: resolving in subshells that throw their cache away, per-name `grep -R` loops, bash string surgery on 70 KB files, and fork-per-line helpers. The box has 12 vCPU and the suite uses one.

The self-compile allocates 361.8 MB and keeps all of it, since nothing reclaims (`lib/ports/process.port:20-24`). By stage: read 7.7 MB, load 60.0, the rest of the front half 30.3, the back half 142.5, emit 121.3. Inside emit, `emit-program` takes 60.7 MB and `bcat-all` takes 27.1 MB to build a 1.26 MB result. `lower-defs` appends to its accumulator once per def at `lib/lowering/compile-back.chiral:272`, which re-copies the list each time. A stage reset boundary is `memory-discipline/M2` (`E82`, `docs/arcs/memory-discipline-arc.md:79`). It needs every survivor of a stage allocated outside the stage's region, a per-site decision, so the stage split above prices it without making it a day.

## Missed specifying

Thirteen items no roster row or finding names. Rows 1 to 9, 11 and 12 above, plus two that are larger than a day:

- **The suite runs on one core of twelve.** Phases share nothing but the tree they read; each makes its own `mktemp -d`. The CPU left after items 2 to 6 is about 200 s, and the box scaled 3x at best on independent compiles, so a memory-aware scheduler would put the suite near 90 s, estimated. It is more than a day for two measured reasons. A `-P 4` attempt drove the box into `Error 24` on every process for about a minute (log, 2026-09-29), and the cause is still unknown. Phase 19 peaks at 1,986 MB, so the scheduler has to know each phase's peak before it can place it.
- **Three stale statements that price the wrong thing.** `tools/test/run-tests.sh:375-377` reads "four generations at about 40 s each" for a phase measured at 15.6 s whole. `bin/chirality-resolve.sh:84-87` and FT-01 rest on resolve costing 2.2x the compile; today it costs 0.6x. FT-02 names Phase 7 as the cache's one beneficiary while `run-tests.sh:181` defeats it. A revisit of FT-01 and FT-02 against the log is owed.

Two latent defects surfaced beside the items. The five census loops interpolate a definition name into an extended regex, so a name holding `?`, `+` or `*` would match the wrong thing; none of the 101 names in `lib/typing/diag.chiral` does today, and the one-pass form in item 3 removes the class. `tools/test/mutant.sh:101` says `mut_slurp` keeps trailing newlines, and both callers strip them.

## Not worth it, with why

- **A compile cache within one suite run.** 17 of 340 logged compiles repeated an input, 4.2 s of 47.2 s. A cache keyed on the compiler's bytes and the blob's bytes is sound without a digest (verify each hit with `cmp` against the stored blob), which answers FT-03's "unconstructible" for the harness tier. It still buys under 2% of the suite.
- **A compile cache across runs.** Every root imports `prelude`, so any `lib/` edit misses almost every entry, and edits to `lib/` are the runs that matter.
- **Running only the phases a change touches.** Every phase compiles through `lib/`, so the dependency set is the whole tree for nearly every edit. Getting it exact is the invalidation proof the rule asks for, and getting it wrong skips a gate.
- **Huge pages for the arena.** `sys` is 0.12 to 0.15 s of a 1.2 s self-compile, and the entry stub is pinned byte for byte (`lib/lowering/compile-emit.chiral:33-50`), so the change costs more than the 10% it could return on the largest compiles.
- **Rewriting the resolver in pure bash.** Measured slower, 660 ms to 1294 ms, and recorded at `bin/chirality-resolve.sh:100-107`.
- **Stage regions for the compiler (`E82`) as a one-day item.** The stage split in the log says it is the largest memory lever, 361.8 MB to roughly the back half's 142.5 MB plus survivors. Every allocation site has to be placed in the right region, and that is the arc's M-to-L sizing. A scalar-result rewind around a per-file loop, a heap mark and reset whose body may return only an `I64`, is its smaller proper slice and would take phase 19's 1,986 MB to its largest file's share. It is more than a day because the rule that nothing allocated inside survives is the work, and that rule has to be a type check. `docs/benchmarks/OPT-CANDIDATES-2026-09.md:378` carries it as D6, absent.
