# Route attack axes: the ceremonial route with per-route rotating tags

The author asked on 2026-09-23: *"per route with the correct engine on both ends
can provide legitimate security right? lets look at this as a table of attack
axis"*, and corrected the shape mid-run: *"Who talks to whom has a ton of axis in
a normal system and still many in a chirality optimal."*

This is an analysis of one construction. It settles nothing, rules on none of the
open calls, and recommends no published scheme. Where the tree supports a
property, the cell names the mechanism and cites the line. Where it does not, the
cell reads **`UNGROUNDED`**, which is an honest result and the most valuable
column in the file.

## The construction under analysis

Recovered verbatim at `.planning/CRYPTO-ROUTING-RECOVERY.md:454-512`, which is
the primary source for everything below.

| piece | what it is |
|---|---|
| the shared secret | sender and hop share `K` from the ceremony, `.planning/CRYPTO-ROUTING-RECOVERY.md:454` |
| the per-packet key | the sender picks a fresh nonce `N`, derives `k = KDF(K, N)`, seals that hop's record under `k`; the hop derives the same `k` and opens it, `:456-458` |
| the wire saving | `N` at 8 to 16 bytes stands where a ~1.1 KB post-quantum ciphertext would, `:460` |
| the obstruction | the hop has to know which `K` to use, `:466` |
| the answer | pre-issued rotating tags with a hop-side lookahead table, O(1) lookup at the cost of state at the hop, `:473`, the shape QUIC's `NEW_CONNECTION_ID` ships, `:475-478` |
| the sizes | per-hop record tag 16 + sealed 16 + MAC 16 = 48 B, header ~384 B at `H` of 8 with no blinding element, ~132 B at radio `H` of 3, `:504`; hop state ~1 MB at a 64-tag lookahead over 1000 sessions, `:507-508` |
| the author's move | state bound goes from per session to **per route**, one entry per route crossing a hop, bounded by topology instead of by traffic, `:567-572` |

The tracked tree carries the conclusion and none of the steps.
`.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:633` holds the wire row *"ceremonial, rotating tag | 8 |
2048 B | ~384 B, **no blinding element** | ~2.4 KB"*, `:640-641` compresses the
whole derivation to *"the per-packet key comes from a route key plus a nonce and
no element is carried"*, and `:560` holds the corner row. Items 1, 2 and 3 of
`.planning/CRYPTO-ROUTING-RECOVERY.md:1084-1086` record that absence.

**Every size in this file carries the VERIFY caveat.**
`.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:528-533` states it: egress is blocked from this sandbox,
so the primitive sizes are from memory and carry the caveat
`docs/decisions/decision-inspiration-policy.md` puts on its license floor. Two
figures are measured rather than recalled, and
`.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:569-571` names them: HORNET's
300+ byte header and Sphinx's 2048 byte fixed payload.

## How to read a cell

| column | rule |
|---|---|
| axis | the attacker's goal |
| the attacker | what they observe or control |
| what the construction gives | the property, and the mechanism that makes it hold. A cell naming an adjective and no mechanism is the failure this run exists to avoid |
| what it does not give | the residue, flat |
| what is bounded, and by what mechanism | §2b only. A bound is the whole achievable result there, and the mechanism is the cell |
| what stays exposed, and is accepted | §2b only. An accepted exposure is an honest cell rather than a gap, and a cell here written as work a roster row could do is wrong |
| grounded at | `file:line`, or `UNGROUNDED` |
| depends on | the roster row or open call the property waits on. Nothing here is settled |

**The tether has its own table, and the reason is that the question there is a
different question.** Over IP the fabric route is hidden and the IP layer
underneath is not. §2a asks what the construction achieves on a native medium,
where a residue is a gap a roster row can close. §2b asks what is bounded and
what is accepted, because `.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:178` says of the three rows
it names that *"none is fixable by cryptography"*. Stapling IP's leak to a native
row makes the native picture look worse than it is and makes the IP picture look
fixable, which it is not.

`.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:174-178` states the whole gap as three rows, link
adjacency exposed to the IP infrastructure, membership exposed, attribution
exposed because an address was issued to someone. The tree names that class and
names no individual IP-layer vector inside it, so an `UNGROUNDED` in §2b for ARP,
DHCP, NAT or an access point binding says the tree covers the class at `:174-178`
and never reaches the vector, which is **a missing sentence rather than a missing
mechanism**.

## §2a · Level 3, half one: the conventional stack, on a native medium

One row per linkage vector a normal system leaks, against the native case: a shm
region, a serial line, radio, and the Ethernet broadcast domain the boundary
question below covers. **Here the question is what the construction achieves, so
a residue is a real gap a roster row can close.**

The verdict column says **removed structurally**, **narrowed**, **replaced with
something narrower**, or **an equivalent remains**.

**What makes a verdict structural.** The idiom is the tree's own, stated at
`.planning/OWN-WEB-GAP.md:89`: *"no source field on the wire | it removes the
authority and the lookup in one move"*, and the reason it survives translation is
that *"a constructor with no source field cannot be given one"*. A cell reads
**removed structurally** only where the absence is a property of a type, a
constructor or a medium that exists, or of a field set specified in a tracked
file such that the field cannot be added without changing it. Where the absence
rests on a design intention, on an unbuilt module, or on an assertion nothing
measures, the verdict is **narrowed** and the cell names what narrowed it.

**Every structural verdict below but one is specified and unbuilt.**
`.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:745` records *"`Mark`, `Parcel`, `Bus`, `Way`, `route`,
the verbs | nothing"*, and `.planning/OWN-WEB-GAP.md:63-65` grepped `lib/` and
`prog/` on 2026-09-05 for zero hits on an address type, an identity bundle and a
destination. So a structural cell here names a record the tree specifies and no
code constructs, which is a claim about a specification. The exception is the
source-address row, where the absence is a property of the medium and holds today

## §2b · Level 3, half one: IP beneath, the tether

**IP's exposures are not overcomeable. The work is to bound them and to state
them.** `.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:178` says it of the three rows it names:
*"The last three rows are the whole gap and none is fixable by cryptography."* So
a cell here records a bound or an acceptance. Neither is a gap a roster row
closes, and a cell written as though a roster row could close NAT or BGP
adjacency would be wrong.

**The model of a bounded exposure is `:180-184`.** With constant-rate fixed-size
tethers an observer sees traffic that never varies with what anyone is doing, so
IP's correlation becomes a **topology disclosure** instead of a **flow
disclosure**: the link graph and membership leak, and no flow on the graph does.
Every bounded cell below has that shape, and where an exposure has no such bound
the cell says so.

**The author has already ruled once on this table.** Per-hop ephemeral IP
addressing was dropped on 2026-09-05 at `:778-785`, because it cannot touch
attribution, an address being issued and issuance being attribution, and because
rotation *"chips at what is left for a cost the property does not repay"*. That
is the tree's record that these exposures were priced and accepted rather than
overlooked.

## §6 · "per route with the correct engine on both ends can provide legitimate security right? lets look at this as a table of attack axis"

Yes for a specific and checkable set of levels, and the yes does not extend to
the levels the question sounds like it is asking about.

**What per route buys on its own.** Level 5 and the bitwise half of level 3: the
per-packet record is fresh under `k = KDF(K, N)` so successive packets do not
correlate by their bits, the payload stays sealed under a separate layer, the
hop sees one undifferentiated stream it cannot split into senders, and a recorded
packet carries no public-key material for a later quantum adversary because `N`
at 8 to 16 bytes replaced the ~1.1 KB ciphertext. Statelessness is what was sold
to buy that, and the per-route move is what kept the price affordable by bounding
the hop's state by topology instead of by traffic.

Every one of those is a property of a specification.
`.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:745` records `Mark`, `Parcel`, `Bus`, `Way`, `route`
and the verbs as nothing in the tree, so the yes is a yes about a design and not
about a binary, and §2a marks which verdicts that narrows.

**What it buys only in combination.** Levels 1 and 2, and the who-talks-to-whom
half of level 3 against any observer wider than one hop, because those are bought
by the cover and the cadence, and the construction supplies neither.

**What it does not reach at all.** Level 4, which content addressing hands out by
construction, and an adversary at both ends.
`.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:470-473` is the whole answer to the question's shape:
all three defences or none of them matter, and HORNET cannot beat an adversary at
both ends **despite being cryptographically sound**.

**What no engine reaches, on either end.** Two things sit outside the layer the
question is about, and the split into §2a and §2b is what makes them legible.

| | |
|---|---|
| **IP beneath** | §2b. Link adjacency, membership and attribution are exposed under a tether and `.planning/REACH-MODEL.md:178` states that none of the three is fixable by cryptography. The cadence converts the loss from a flow disclosure into a topology disclosure at `:180-184` and does not remove it. A correct engine on both ends of a route is the wrong layer to reach any of them, and the honest work there is to bound and to state |
| **the identity layer** | §2b's closing table. If a party's address is derived from its public material, an observer holding both parties' bundles confirms the pair by recomputation wherever an address-derived value is observable, and the tree prints one at `:688` and asks by one at `:723-724`. The engine sits at the wrong layer to answer it, and the tree answers it nowhere |

So the honest form of the yes is that the engine removes the cryptographic ways
to lose, leaves the traffic-analysis ways standing waiting on `R7` and `R8`, and
never touches the tether's three exposures or the matching property one layer up.
